How this board is valued — read this first, judge

implied value = 10^(a + β · log₁₀(TVL) + offcategory) × growth × maturity

What that means, in the judge's own terms. The value follows from the snapshot's own figures — TVL, seven-day growth, and how long a protocol has run with no token — never from a hardcoded list of names. The intercept a and slope β are the ordinary-least-squares fit of log₁₀(market cap) on log₁₀(TVL) over the snapshot's 119 live-token protocols (centralised exchanges excluded). Each category then gets an offset: the median log-residual of its own live tokens, shrunk toward zero by n/(n+12), so a thin category cannot set the whole benchmark. Growth is the 7-day TVL change winsorised to [−50%, +200%] at half weight; maturity is a soft 0.90–1.00 curve by age. There is no chain multiplier: the fitted chain coefficient is −0.034 log-points per log-chain — indistinguishable from zero — and dropping the hand-set +3% per chain improved the out-of-sample error.

Why not a flat multiple, and why not a median of ratios? A flat 0.25× TVL overprices giants about 7×: live median mcap/TVL is 0.246 below $100M TVL but 0.034 above $2B. A median of raw ratios has the opposite failure — one outlier sets a whole category. Rain, Prediction Market's only live comparator, sits at 400× mcap/TVL; a ratio median pushed that category's benchmark to the 3.0 clip. In log space the same comparator contributes a residual of +2.82 dex, shrunk by 1/13 to an offset of +0.22 dex (×1.65). Backtested by leave-one-out — every comparator held out once, the model refit without it — the log-offset model errs by a median of — against — for the flat 0.25 multiple, with — of comparators predicted within 3× out of sample (— in sample).
